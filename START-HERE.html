Website Handbook

Your site, and how to run it.

Everything is built. Nothing here requires knowing how to code — work top to bottom and you’ll be live in a few minutes.

1

Put it online

About two minutes
  1. Unzip the download. Inside raven-wikle-website you’ll find this handbook and a folder called website. The website folder is your whole site — it’s the only thing you ever upload.
  2. Go to app.netlify.com/drop and make a free account.
  3. Drag the website folder onto the drop area.
  4. Wait about thirty seconds. Netlify hands you a live address like gentle-marsh-48201.netlify.app.
  5. Under Site configuration → Change site name, rename it to something like raven-wikle-real-estate.

Using your own domain

In Netlify, go to Domain management → Add a domain. It walks you through the steps and issues a free security certificate. If you already own the domain, you’ll point its nameservers at Netlify — their instructions are step by step.

Once your real domain is connected, open website/sitemap.xml and website/robots.txt and swap ravenwiklerealestate.netlify.app for it. Optional, but it helps Google find you.

2

Turn on lead notifications

About five minutes · do this first

Two switches in Netlify connect your forms to your inbox. Do them in this order.

First: switch on form detection

  1. In Netlify, open your site and click Forms in the left menu.
  2. Click Enable form detection.
  3. Upload the website folder again, the way step 5 describes under “Publishing your changes.” Netlify only looks for forms during an upload, so this step is required.
  4. Refresh the Forms page. You should see all seven forms listed by name.

Then: send submissions to your email

  1. Still under Forms, open Submission notifications.
  2. Click Add notification → Email notification.
  3. Email to notify: raven@ravenashleyhomes.com
  4. Form: choose Any form to get all seven in one place.
  5. Save.

Test it

Fill out the Buyers form on your live site with your own details. Within a minute or two you should get an email from formresponses@netlify.com with the subject “New buyer lead from your website.” Each form has its own subject line, so you can tell leads apart at a glance.

Hit Reply and it goes straight to the person who filled out the form, not to Netlify.

No email? Check your spam folder first, and mark it “not spam” so the next one lands in your inbox. If it isn’t there either, check that the seven forms appear under Forms — if they don’t, form detection is off or the site hasn’t been uploaded again since you turned it on.

3

Automatic thank-you email

Optional · about ten minutes · free

Right now someone who fills out a form lands on your thank-you page. If you’d also like them to get a branded email from you within seconds:

  1. Make a free account at resend.com.
  2. Add and verify ravenashleyhomes.com. They show you exactly which records to add.
  3. Copy your API key.
  4. In Netlify, go to Site configuration → Environment variables and add two: 
    RESEND_API_KEY = re_xxxxxxxxxxxxx
FROM_EMAIL     = Raven Wikle <raven@ravenashleyhomes.com>
  5. Redeploy under Deploys → Trigger deploy.

The wording of that email lives in netlify/functions/submission-created.mjs, in plain English near the top, if you ever want to change it.

Skipping this breaks nothing. Your forms work exactly the same either way.

4

Adding IDX search later

When your provider is approved

Your Search Homes page currently sends visitors to the two official MLS searches — REIN and CBRAR. When you’re ready for a real embedded search on your own site:

  1. Sign up with a provider that covers your boards. IDX Broker, iHomefinder and Showcase IDX all support REIN; IDX Broker also lists CBRAR.
  2. They’ll need an IDX agreement signed by ReNaye Dame plus approval from each board. That’s normal, and usually takes a few business days per board.
  3. They give you a short snippet of code.
  4. Open website/search.html and find this line near the top: 
    <div id="idx-slot"></div>
  5. Paste their code between the tags: 
    <div id="idx-slot">
  PASTE THE IDX CODE HERE
</div>
  6. Upload the website folder again. The search section appears on its own.

Until then the section stays hidden, so the page looks finished either way.

5

Editing the site yourself

No special software needed

Every page lives in the public folder. Open any .html file in TextEdit, Notepad, or a free editor like VS Code. Anything you’d want to change is marked with a comment.

To change words, find the sentence and type over it. Leave anything inside angle brackets alone.

Adding a new listing

Your listings live in the site files, not in a Netlify setting, so you add one by editing a file and uploading the folder again.

  1. Work from your newest copy. Use the website folder from the most recent zip, or one you’ve already edited. Uploading an older copy would undo later changes.
  2. Add the photos first. Put them in website/assets/img/, named by house number, as described just below.
  3. Open website/listings.html in a text editor. On a Mac, TextEdit shows HTML files as a formatted page unless you first go to TextEdit → Settings → Open and Save and tick Display HTML files as HTML code instead of formatted text. On Windows, right-click the file and choose Open with → Notepad. The free VS Code works on both and is easier to read.
  4. Find the listing block. Search the file (Cmd+F or Ctrl+F) for <article class="listing. The block runs from that line down to the next </article>, about fourteen lines.
  5. Copy the whole block and paste it right below that </article>. Put your newest listing first, since the top one shows largest.
  6. Edit the copy. Change these lines and leave everything else alone: 
    <span class="listing__tag">Active</span>
<img src="assets/img/listing-1234.jpg" alt="1234 Main Street, Gloucester, VA 23061" ...
<span class="listing__price">$000,000</span>
<p class="listing__addr">1234 Main Street<br>Gloucester, VA 23061</p>
<p class="listing__note">A short line: waterfront, acreage, new roof</p>
<span>3 Beds</span><span>2 Baths</span><span>1,800 Sq Ft</span>
  7. Fix the buttons. The line starting <div class="btn-row" points at the Tyndall Drive page. In your copy, change both 7534-tyndall-drive.html links to buyers.html#connect, so visitors go to your contact form. (If you’d like a full page for the new home like Tyndall Drive has, send me the details and I’ll build it.)
  8. Save, then check your work. Double-click listings.html to open it in your browser. You’ll see exactly what visitors will see, before anything goes live.
  9. Homepage too, if you like. website/index.html has the same block under “Currently on the market.” Repeat steps 4–8 there.

Status changes are one word. When a listing goes under contract, change Active to Under Contract. When it closes, delete the block and add the address to Recent sales below.

Adding listing photos

Put the photos in website/assets/img/ and name them by house number. For 7534 Tyndall Drive:

  • listing-7534.jpg — the cover photo. Shows on the listings page, the homepage and the top of the listing page. Use your best exterior.
  • listing-7534-2.jpg through listing-7534-17.jpg — the gallery on the listing page, in that order. Visitors can click any photo to see it full-screen and arrow through the rest.

Each photo appears on its own once it’s in the folder. Any number you skip simply doesn’t show, and the gallery stays hidden until there’s at least one. Landscape photos around 1400 pixels wide work best.

Adding a past sale

In website/listings.html, find the <ol class="sales"> list. Each sale is one <li class="sale"> block — copy one and change the address, city, price, beds, baths, square footage, and which side you represented.

Adding a review

In website/testimonials.html, copy a <blockquote class="quote"> block and replace the words and the name.

Changing a color everywhere

Open website/assets/css/style.css. The first twenty lines define every color on the site. Change one there and it updates across every page.

Publishing your changes

  1. Log in to app.netlify.com and click your site.
  2. Click Deploys in the left menu.
  3. Scroll to the bottom of that page, to the box that invites you to drag and drop your site folder.
  4. Drag your website folder onto it. It’s live in about twenty seconds, at the same address.

Don’t use app.netlify.com/drop for updates. That page creates a brand-new site with a new address. Updates always go on your existing site’s Deploys page. If something looks wrong after an update, the same Deploys page lists every earlier version — click one and choose Publish deploy to roll back.

6

Your local fall guide

A lead magnet that works while you sleep

Your fall guide now lives on the site in two forms. The full calendar is readable at fall-guide.html, which is the version Google indexes — people search “pumpkin patch near Gloucester” every October, and now they can land on your site. The printable two-page PDF sits behind a short form, so downloading it puts a name and email in your Netlify inbox.

Both live under Local in your menu, alongside a page about the area. That grouping leaves room for a spring guide, neighborhood pages or market updates later without the menu growing every season.

The PDF

It’s at website/assets/raven-wikle-fall-guide-2026.pdf, rebuilt in your navy and light blue with your logo on the cover and your full contact details on the back. When someone forwards it to a friend who’s thinking about moving here, your name is on it.

Updating it next season

The events are written directly into website/fall-guide.html. Each one is a block that starts with <article class="event"> — copy one, paste it, and change the month, day, weekday, title, hours, address and description. Delete the ones that have passed.

The PDF won’t update itself. It’s a separate file. When the calendar changes enough to matter, send me the new events and I’ll rebuild both together so they match.

Where to point people

Link to the guide page rather than the PDF when you post it on Instagram or hand it out — that way the download form does its job. The short address is yoursite.com/fall-guide.
